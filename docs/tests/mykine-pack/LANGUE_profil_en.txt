Événement : 2025-11-16 — Le prototype rate une répétition sur trois sur l'iPhone 11
TITRE : The iPhone 11 test

The laptop counted every squat. The iPhone 11 missed one out of three.

On November 16, I tested the first MyKiné prototype with Julien’s phone instead of my development machine.

The page was a small web app using MediaPipe Pose in JavaScript. It counted a squat when the hip-knee-ankle angle went below 100°.

On my laptop, that was enough for a convincing demo.

On Julien’s iPhone 11, the page ran at 6 fps. Julien still did 20 squats in his living room so I could compare the counter with a known number.

The counter missed one repetition out of three.

That was not a model-tuning detail. It was a device constraint: the same page and the same squat logic behaved differently when the camera stream ran on a phone.

I had no fix that day. I had a reproducible limit, measured on the device, with the exercise and the number of repetitions known.

The laptop demo had shown that the idea could work. The iPhone 11 showed that the prototype was not ready for a real session.

Événement : 2025-11-23 — Le traitement est déplacé sur le téléphone
TITRE : The frame never left the phone

The server added 300–600 ms to every squat check on Julien’s 4G. It also left images of him in his underwear on my VPS.

I had moved the frames to the server because the iPhone 11 prototype was running at 6 fps and missing one repetition out of three. The server looked like the obvious way to get more compute.

The test killed that assumption. A health exercise is happening in someone’s home, on an ordinary mobile connection. Sending every camera frame out creates a delay the user can feel, and creates a copy of the most sensitive data I have.

So on November 23, I moved the treatment onto the phone: camera frames stay on the device, the model runs there, and the server does not receive the video.

That simplified the privacy boundary and removed the network dependency. It did not solve the vision problem: on Julien’s iPhone 11, the prototype still ran at 6 fps and missed one repetition out of three.

The trade-off was now explicit. I had to make the model and the mobile runtime work within the phone’s limits instead of hiding the problem behind a server.

The next failure was already waiting: when Julien trembled at the bottom of a squat, one repetition was counted three times.

Événement : 2025-12-06 — Le tremblement fait compter trois fois une répétition
TITRE : When a correct angle counts three squats

On December 6, a knee trembling at the bottom of a squat made MyKiné count one repetition three times.

The angle was calculated correctly. I used the dot product of the hip-to-knee and ankle-to-knee vectors.

The counter had two thresholds:

above 160° meant the leg was high;
below 100° meant it was low.

When the knee trembled near the bottom, the angle crossed the 100° boundary three times. The counter treated those crossings as three separate repetitions.

That made the failure more revealing: the measurement could be right while the repetition logic was wrong. The problem was in the sequence of states, not in the angle itself.

The model was already running on the phone. That avoided sending camera frames to a server, but it did not solve this counting failure.

I did not correct it that day. MyKiné could measure the knee angle. It still could not reliably tell one squat from three.
